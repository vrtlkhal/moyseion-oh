The Magna Graecia houses

7th – 4th century BCE

From the 8th century BC, the cities of ancient Greece began to scatter their seeds across the western Mediterranean, casting their gaze upon the sunlit shores of southern Italy and Sicily.

These new colonies blossomed into vibrant realms, their hearts pulsing with political power and military strength, yet equally alive with the flame of culture and the arts.

In these lands dwelled masterful artisans, gifted ceramists, and visionary artists; poets whose verses echoed through time, scientists and philosophers who sought to unravel the mysteries of existence, and playwrights who gave voice to the human soul.

From their collective brilliance arose an extraordinary civilization—Μεγάλη Ἑλλάς (Magna Graecia)—a beacon that carried the spirit of Greek culture to the farthest reaches of the West.

Here, the red-figure pottery flourished, including the exquisite Apulian-Lucanian styles that graced the Lucanian landscape.

The tombs held treasures of immense beauty, and the gold filigree jewelry, wrought by the hands of Magna Graecia's artisans, shimmered with an elegance that defied the ages.
